The full breakdown

Your profit calculator only sees part of the fees.

A TikTok seller can do $50,000 in a month — and keep $1,125 of it. That is not a guess. That is the math once every fee layer is counted: referral fees, transaction and processing fees, shipping, affiliate commissions, Shop Ads, content production, returns, and operations. The visible platform fees are only about 10% of revenue. The other 57% is scattered across dashboards no one reads together — so sellers price from the visible number and find out the truth only when the payout lands.

And if you sell on Amazon, Etsy, or eBay too, it is the same problem with different names. Amazon's 15% referral fee plus fulfilment and storage. Etsy's transaction fee plus Offsite Ads that can double the fee on one order. eBay's final value fee charged on shipping and tax too. Every marketplace advertises a low headline fee and collects the real money elsewhere — and no calculator you already use sees all of it, because it was never built to.

I am building this for an agency — a custom app for sellers called the Fee-Stack Calculator. One core, one module per marketplace — TikTok Shop, Amazon, Etsy, and eBay. The seller connects their shop account, and the app scans their real orders — pulling every cost layer into one live margin dashboard that shows true profit per order, per product, and per marketplace. Before a seller lists or reprices anything, the app runs the full stack against the proposed price and warns when that price loses money. Pricing stops being a guess and becomes a decision made with the whole stack in view.

Why the calculators sellers already use miss it

Most sellers already use a profit calculator. That is exactly the problem. The calculators they have fail in three ways: they are manual — the seller types numbers into a spreadsheet and gets a snapshot that is a month old within a week; they only see the visible fees, about 10% of revenue on TikTok Shop, while the other 57% lives in other dashboards the calculator never touches; and they report after the fact, telling you what last month's profit was instead of stopping you before you list a product at a price that loses money. Sell on more than one marketplace and the gap gets worse, not better — fees scattered across three platforms, three ads dashboards and three returns queues, and no single calculator that sees all of them.

The four fee stacks, verified

TikTok Shop — the full worked example. From Darkroom Agency's 2026 fee breakdown for a fashion brand doing $50,000 a month (1,250 orders at a $40 average order value): referral fee 8% ($4,000), transaction fees ($375), payment processing 2.0% ($1,000), shipping and fulfilment ~10% ($5,000), affiliate commissions ~10% blended ($5,000), TikTok Shop Ads ~20% ($10,000), content production ($4,000), returns and chargebacks ~5% ($2,500), operational overhead ($2,000) — total selling costs $33,875, or 67.75% of revenue. After a 30% product cost ($15,000), the seller keeps $1,125: a 2.25% net margin. The visible platform fees alone are only $5,375, or 10.75%. The other $28,500 is the fee stack.

Amazon — the published per-order example. A $29.99 kitchen gadget on FBA: referral fee 15% ($4.50), FBA fulfilment ~$4.75, fuel and logistics surcharge 3.5% (~$0.17), monthly storage ~$0.15 — about $9.57, or 32% of the price, before product cost, Sponsored Products ad spend and returns. Amazon's stack is dominated by the referral fee and the per-unit fulfilment fee, plus storage that triples in Q4.

Etsy — the published per-order example. A $30 item with free shipping, US seller, no ads: listing fee $0.20, transaction fee 6.5% ($1.95), payment processing 3% + $0.25 ($1.15) — $3.30, or 11% of the order. If that sale comes through an Offsite Ad at 15%, another $4.50 goes to Etsy and the total becomes $7.80, or 26% of the order. The transaction fee applies to shipping too, and the processing fee to the full order total including tax.

eBay — illustrative arithmetic from published rates. A $50 sale in a typical category: final value fee 13.6% ($6.80) plus a $0.40 per-order fee — $7.20, or 14.4% of the sale; add a 5% Promoted Listings rate and it becomes $9.70, or 19.4%. The trap: the final value fee is charged on the whole amount the buyer pays, including shipping and sales tax.

The app, piece by piece

  1. Unified cost model — every cost, from every marketplace, stored the same way: per order, per SKU, per marketplace, per month — so a SKU sold on two platforms can be compared side by side.
  2. Live margin dashboard — the headline number is not revenue, it is true margin: what the seller actually keeps, updated as orders, fees and ad spend change.
  3. Warn-before-you-price simulator — run the full stack for a marketplace against a proposed price before listing, and get warned when the price loses money.
  4. Cross-marketplace view — one screen showing which platform actually keeps the most per order on the same product, so the next batch of stock goes where the margin is.
  5. One fee adapter per marketplace — TikTok Shop partner and seller APIs, Amazon's Selling Partner API, the Etsy API, and eBay's developer APIs, each translating that platform's fees into the shared model, verified against one real statement before the next adapter is added.

The business impact, with the math

Here is the $50,000 month, line by line: $4,000 referral fees, $375 transaction fees, $1,000 processing, $5,000 shipping, $5,000 affiliate commissions, $10,000 ads, $4,000 content, $2,500 returns, $2,000 operations — $33,875 in selling costs, 67.75% of revenue. After product cost, $1,125 is left. A 2.25% margin on a $50,000 month.

And here is what changes when the seller can see the stack — an illustrative scenario, not a real client's result: reprice the SKUs that were selling below true cost, cut the ad campaigns whose ROAS cannot cover real margin — and the same $50,000 month keeps $5,125 instead. Same sales. Same volume. Just decisions made with the numbers visible first. The calculator does not create sales. It stops a seller from giving away the sales they already have.

The build roadmap

This guide is the blueprint on which the app is being worked from: write the prompt as the spec, build the front end and dashboard with a vibe-coding platform (v0, Lovable, Bolt or Replit), build the shared core first and then one marketplace adapter at a time, connect the real data per marketplace, build the price simulator and test it against three real SKUs, deploy on Vercel or Netlify, then run the app for one month beside the old method and compare the margin figure against the actual payout. The guide finishes with the launch checklist: every fee layer listed per marketplace, one real month's statement totalled per marketplace, true profit per order computed for the three highest-volume SKUs, SKUs below true cost flagged for repricing, affiliate rates and ad budgets set against true margin, and the price simulator run on every new listing before it goes live.
