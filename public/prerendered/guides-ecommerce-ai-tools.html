The Ecommerce AI Tools Guide

I found a useful repo that helps ecommerce sellers. It is on GitHub — nexscope-ai/ecommerce-ai-tools — and it is the rare one that is actually built for sellers, not developers. Three tools inside it, all free to browse: product videos, marketplace data, and a free product page. This guide covers what is inside it, how you can use it, and the app I will build out of it.

The full breakdown

I went through GitHub's new AI repos so sellers don't have to. Most of them are built for developers — coding agents, agent skills, phone harnesses. This one is actually built for sellers: nexscope-ai/ecommerce-ai-tools. I read the docs, compared the five video workflows, and checked the data directory before writing this guide.

The gap it closes is the listing itself. A seller's listing usually fails in three quiet places at once. First, the product has no video — a photo sits on the listing while every feed around it moves. Second, the keywords are guessed — the seller writes what they think buyers search, instead of checking what buyers actually search and say. Third, the product is invisible to AI — search engines and AI systems have no clean public page to read, so they cannot find it, quote it, or recommend it. None of those three is a sales problem. They are listing problems, and they are all fixable with the same three tools.

Tool one — product videos: five workflows

Image to Video from your product photos, URL to Video from a listing link, Avatar Video with a presenter, Video Clone from a reference clip, and Bulk Video for a whole batch at once. You compare the workflows and watch the official examples before you generate anything. The directory and the examples are free to browse; video generation uses credits, so check the estimate in the selected workspace before generating.

Tool two — marketplace data: six marketplaces, one directory

One API directory for Amazon, TikTok, 1688, Shopify, Shopee and Walmart — product search, reviews, sourcing, keywords, marketplace research. You find the data for the task, compare the operations, read the official docs, and test before you integrate. The rule is simple: find the data for the task before you build anything on it.

Tool three — a free product page

The Product Gallery gives your product a public page — facts, images, optional video, and your store link — built for search and AI discovery, so AI systems can find and cite what you sell. Submission, review and listing are completely free. No payment, no store account connection. Your product stays offline until it is reviewed and approved.

The app, piece by piece

This is how I will use the repo to build the app out of it — one dashboard where video, data and discovery live together, not three tabs:

  1. Video queue — every listing's photos turned into videos, batch by batch, through the five workflows.
  2. Keyword + review watch — your listings tracked against real marketplace data, so keywords stop being guesses and reviews stop being unread.
  3. Product page live — your products listed on the free public page, built for search and AI discovery.
  4. One dashboard — video, data and discovery in the same place: which listings have a video, which keywords need fixing, and which products are live in the gallery.

How you can use it

Pick your best photo, open the video workflows, compare them, watch an example, and make one video. Then check your keywords and reviews in the data directory — find your platform and read what buyers actually say. Then list the product free in the gallery and get a public page built for search and AI. Start with one listing. One listing done properly beats ten half-done.

The honest cost

The directories and guides are free to browse. Video generation uses credits, so check the estimate in the workspace before you generate. API requests follow each operation's access and credit requirements. The gallery is $0.

The build roadmap

This guide is the blueprint on which the app is being worked from: write the prompt as the spec, build the front end and dashboard with a vibe-coding platform (v0, Lovable, Bolt or Replit), build the video queue first and the keyword watch second, connect the real data through the ecommerce Data APIs and the Product Gallery, test the queue against three real listings at different stages, deploy on Vercel or Netlify, then run the app for one month beside the old method and compare the listings with video, keywords fixed, and products live in the gallery against the month before. Download the guide above and try it on one listing first.
